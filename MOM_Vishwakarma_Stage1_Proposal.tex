\section{Problem Statement}

\noindent
\framebox[\textwidth][s]{%
\parbox{0.97\textwidth}{%
\vspace{0.3em}
\textbf{Problem Statement (in one statement):}\\
Over 80\% of rural primary health centers in India lack continuous intrapartum fetal monitoring because existing ultrasound cardiotocography (CTG) systems are cost-prohibitive (\$2,500--\$8,000 USD), physically tethered, and require continuous manual beam re-aiming by skilled ultrasound technicians, leaving frontline clinicians blind to intrapartum fetal hypoxia and resulting in preventable stillbirths and neonatal brain damage.
\vspace{0.3em}
}%
}

\vspace{0.6em}
\noindent
\textbf{Biophysical and Clinical Background:} Intrapartum fetal distress---primarily acute asphyxia and umbilical cord compression---is the leading cause of intrapartum stillbirths and neonatal hypoxic-ischemic encephalopathy (HIE). While ultrasound CTG serves as the urban hospital gold standard, its acoustic Doppler principle fails in rural environments: whenever the fetus descends or shifts during active labor, the narrow acoustic beam loses the cardiac focus, necessitating immediate manual repositioning by trained personnel. Non-invasive abdominal biopotential acquisition presents a transformative alternative by capturing the electrical field vector omnidirectionally. However, maternal abdominal skin potentials present an extreme signal separation challenge: the maternal ECG (1000--5000~$\mu$V) is 10 to 100 times stronger than the microvolt fetal ECG (10--50~$\mu$V), heavily corrupted by maternal uterine electromyogram (EMG) shivering, respiratory motion, and 50~Hz powerline hum.

\section{Solution Proposed: Architecture, Features \& Mechanisms}

\noindent
\textbf{Overview:} MOM is an ultra-low-power, wearable maternal-fetal electrophysiological acquisition platform that performs deterministic adaptive noise cancellation directly on an embedded microcontroller at the edge. The system eliminates acoustic ultrasound radiation, replaces gelled transducers with reusable belt electrodes, and continuously extracts Fetal Heart Rate (FHR), maternal heart rate (MHR), Signal Quality Index (SQI), and Electrohysterogram (EHG) uterine contractions without requiring a cloud connection.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.88\textwidth]{docs/assets/bw_slide_02_solution_architecture.png}
    \caption{MOM End-to-End System Topology --- Abdominal electrodes to analog front-end (AFE), MCU DSP engine, and BLE clinical telemetry.}
    \label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure}

\noindent
\textbf{Technical Mechanisms \& Subsystems:}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em]
    \item \textbf{Analog Front-End (AFE):} Built upon the Texas Instruments ADS1298 low-noise, 24-bit delta-sigma ADC with 8 simultaneous differential channels, internal programmable gain amplifiers ($\text{PGA} = 6$), and active Right Leg Drive (RLD) common-mode rejection exceeding $-110$~dB.
    \item \textbf{Edge Processing Unit:} Nordic Semiconductor nRF52840 SoC featuring an ARM Cortex-M4F core clocked at 64~MHz with single-precision hardware FPU, 256~KB SRAM, and 1~MB embedded Flash.
    \item \textbf{Signal Conditioning Pipeline:} Multi-stage digital filtering including 2nd-order Butterworth high-pass ($f_c = 0.5$~Hz) for baseline wander removal, 4th-order Butterworth low-pass ($f_c = 100$~Hz) for EMG attenuation, and a 50~Hz IIR notch filter ($Q = 30$) for powerline interference rejection.
    \item \textbf{Adaptive Separation Engine (32-Tap NLMS):} A reference thoracic lead captures maternal ECG $x[n]$ to adaptively predict maternal abdominal interference $\hat{y}[n] = \mathbf{w}^T[n]\mathbf{x}[n]$. The maternal-cancelled residual error $e[n] = d[n] - \hat{y}[n]$ isolates pure fetal complexes with mathematical convergence:
    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{w}[n+1] = \mathbf{w}[n] + \frac{\mu}{\|\mathbf{x}[n]\|^2 + \epsilon} e[n] \mathbf{x}[n]
    \end{equation}
    operating with filter order $M = 32$, step size $\mu = 0.05$, and regularization $\epsilon = 10^{-8}$.
    \item \textbf{Feature Extraction:} Real-time Pan-Tompkins peak detection extracts beat-to-beat FHR; spectral energy ratio calculates SQI; 0.1--4.0~Hz bandpass with Teager-Kaiser Energy Operator (TKEO) computes uterine contractions.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Safety and Efficiency Analysis}

\noindent
\textbf{Safety by Design:} Unlike traditional ultrasound transducers that continuously emit acoustic energy (raising cavitation and thermal index considerations), MOM operates completely passively by detecting endogenous electrical potentials. Safety features include:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textit{Patient Electrical Isolation:} Isolated ground topology with TVS ESD diodes rated to $\pm 15$~kV air discharge, adhering to IEC 60601-1 Type BF applied part leakage limits ($< 10~\mu$A normal condition).
    \item \textit{Real-Time Lead-Off Detection:} The ADS1298 internal current source comparator automatically detects dislodged electrodes within 1.0~ms, flagging signal invalidity to prevent diagnostic misinterpretation.
    \item \textit{Fail-Safe Local Edge Autonomy:} All filtering, FHR derivation, and alarms are executed on-chip. The system functions autonomously even during complete hospital power blackouts or cellular network loss.
\end{itemize}

\noindent
\textbf{Efficiency Gains:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textit{Material Handling \& Staff Burden:} Eliminates ultrasound coupling gel, belt tightening straps, and continuous manual transducer re-aiming, saving over 40 minutes of nursing labor per laboring patient.
    \item \textit{Ultra-Low Power Draw:} Continuous operating power consumption is under 33~mW ($\sim 9.82$~mA @ 3.3V), enabling over 200 hours of continuous monitoring on a single 2000~mAh rechargeable lithium cell.
\end{itemize}

\section{Scalability and Future Development}

\noindent
\textbf{Four-Tier Healthcare Scalability Path:} MOM scales seamlessly across the entire public healthcare delivery pyramid without requiring specialized infrastructure:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.8em]
    \item \textit{Device Level (Patient Home / Antenatal Ward):} Wearable, lightweight belt worn during ambulation or sleep.
    \item \textit{Clinic Bed Level (Sub-Center / Delivery Cot):} Continuous wireless telemetry streaming to low-cost frontline tablets.
    \item \textit{Primary Health Center (PHC):} Central nursing monitoring station consolidating multi-bed telemetry with automated triage priority scoring.
    \item \textit{District Hospital / Specialist Tier:} Secure gateway forwarding high-risk anomaly records for tele-obstetric specialist review.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=0.88\textwidth]{docs/assets/bw_slide_09_scalability_roadmap.png}
    \caption{Healthcare Scalability Hierarchy --- Device deployment pathway connecting home cots to district referral hospitals.}
    \label{fig:scalability}

\noindent
\textbf{Future Enhancements and Adaptations:}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.5em]
    \item \textit{Multi-Vector Active Lead Selection:} Firmware upgrades to automatically rank and select optimal differential lead pairs based on dynamic Signal Quality Index (SQI), accommodating unexpected fetal rotation.
    \item \textit{Dual-Tier Cloud Research AI:} While edge DSP remains strictly deterministic for on-device safety, an optional hospital-tier 1D-W-NETR Vision Transformer benchmark evaluates retrospective multi-center cohorts for rare morphological anomaly discovery.
    \item \textit{Expansion to High-Risk Ambulatory Triage:} Adaptation of the hardware for outpatient gestational hypertension and preeclampsia tele-monitoring in remote tribal areas.
\end{itemize}

\section{Innovation Advantage: Differentiation from Existing Market Products}

\noindent

By rejecting computationally bloated neural network models at the edge and instead implementing an optimized 32-tap NLMS adaptive filter with CMSIS-DSP SIMD acceleration, MOM achieves hospital-grade signal recovery on an off-the-shelf \$3.50 microcontroller.
